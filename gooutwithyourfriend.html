GO OUT WITH YOUR FRIEND

You realize that you don't remember the last time you saw your friends for more than 5 minutes when you accidentally run into each other at the library or in the neighborhood. And that even in those few times your friends had expressed interest in hanging out, but it was you who had some other-more important-things to do.

The thought makes you acknowledge how bad of a friend you've been in the last few weeks (or rather months).

Although you believe that your friends would understand the circumstances, it is still an unpleasant thought to have.

You take your phone and text your friend who lives a few blocks from you if she wants to grab a drink and play a game or two of pool at the bar where you used to hang out before.

She does not reply immediately so while waiting for her repsonse you are rethinking your choice; maybe it was a bad idea to go out right now, when you can already imagine the consequences, you can imagine how you will feel tomorrow and that most probably you will not be in the most optimal shape to have an early morning and that you can forget about having the most productive day tomorrow.

So as you're sitting on your couch, you are thinking of how to proceed after having had this inner dilemma resurface again:

  • Wait for her response and let her decision guide your night, because youknow that spending time with your friends will help take your mind off your anxieties that you've taken on by yourself, that talking with your friends will relieve the pressure and that simply being with your friends will bring you joy.
  • Delete the message you sent you sent because: what were you thinking? Going out when you are already struggling to manage your anxieties and the various pressures, when you should be prioritizing your work over everything else, when you should be thinking how to put yourself in a position where you would spend your time as efficiently as possible to succeed. Could anything be more irresponsible than going out drinking at this moment?
